Student Finance UK 2026: A Complete Guide to Student Loans, Maintenance & Managing Money

13 August 2026 ·

Starting university can be exciting, but working out how to pay for tuition, rent, food, transport and everyday expenses can feel overwhelming. For many students, student finance in the UK is an important part of making higher education affordable.

In England, eligible students can generally apply for a Tuition Fee Loan to help cover university tuition fees and a Maintenance Loan to help with living costs. The Tuition Fee Loan is normally paid directly to the university, while Maintenance Loan payments are paid into the student's bank account.

But student finance is more than simply borrowing money. Understanding how funding works, creating a realistic budget and knowing what to do if money becomes difficult to manage can help students avoid unnecessary financial pressure.

In this Student Finance UK 2026 guide, we explain how student loans work, what Maintenance Loans are, how to budget as a student and where to look for help if debt becomes difficult to manage.

Key Takeaways

  • Student finance can help eligible students cover tuition fees and living costs.
  • Tuition Fee Loans are normally paid directly to the university.
  • Maintenance Loans are designed to help with living expenses.
  • The amount of Maintenance Loan you receive can depend on household income and where you live.
  • A student loan is different from most ordinary consumer credit.
  • Creating a budget before university can help prevent unnecessary borrowing.
  • Credit cards, overdrafts and Buy Now Pay Later should not automatically be treated as solutions to a regular budget shortfall.
  • If other debts become difficult to manage, getting help early can make your options clearer.

What Is Student Finance in the UK?

Student finance is financial support available to eligible students to help them pay for higher education.

Depending on your circumstances, this can include:

  • Tuition Fee Loans
  • Maintenance Loans
  • Additional grants or allowances in some circumstances
  • Support for students with disabilities or specific financial responsibilities

The exact rules depend on where you live in the UK. England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland operate different student-finance systems, so students should check the rules that apply to their home nation.

For students applying through Student Finance England, GOV.UK currently states that a full-time undergraduate student can receive up to £9,790 for tuition fees for the 2026–27 academic year, subject to eligibility and the applicable rules.

Tuition Fee Loan vs Maintenance Loan

One of the easiest ways to understand student finance is to separate the two main types of support.

Type of funding What it helps with Where the money goes
Tuition Fee LoanUniversity or college tuition feesUsually directly to the university
Maintenance LoanLiving costsUsually directly to the student's bank account
Additional supportSpecific circumstancesDepends on eligibility

A Tuition Fee Loan is intended to cover eligible tuition charges. You normally do not receive this money directly in your bank account.

A Maintenance Loan, on the other hand, is designed to help with day-to-day living costs such as accommodation, food and transport. GOV.UK says the amount can depend on factors including household income and where you live while studying.

How Much Student Finance Can You Get in 2026?

There isn't one universal amount that every student receives.

Your funding can depend on factors such as:

  • Household income
  • Where you live while studying
  • Whether you live with your parents
  • Whether you study in London
  • Your course
  • Your previous study
  • Your personal circumstances
  • Whether you are studying full-time or part-time

This means two students attending the same university can potentially receive different amounts of Maintenance Loan.

Simple Student Finance Breakdown

Think about student finance like this:

Student finance available → Tuition costs + living-cost support

Then your living-cost support needs to cover expenses such as:

Accommodation + food + transport + bills + study costs + personal spending

If your expenses are higher than your available income, you have a budget gap.

That gap is important because repeatedly filling it with credit cards, overdrafts or other borrowing can create a separate debt problem.

What Is a Maintenance Loan?

A Maintenance Loan is intended to help eligible students with living expenses while studying.

Unlike the Tuition Fee Loan, the Maintenance Loan is generally paid directly into your bank account.

You might use it for:

  • Rent
  • Food
  • Electricity and household bills
  • Travel
  • Books and study materials
  • Phone costs
  • Clothing
  • Other reasonable living expenses

However, a Maintenance Loan isn't necessarily designed to cover every expense a student might want to make.

This is why creating a student budget before spending the first payment can be extremely useful.

Example Student Budget

Imagine a student receives £9,000 over an academic year.

A simplified budget could look like this:

Accommodation£5,400
Food£1,500
Transport£500
Phone & subscriptions£300

This is only an illustrative example, not a recommended spending level. Actual costs vary significantly between students and locations.

The important lesson is that a large payment at the beginning of term can look like a lot of money until it is divided across several months.

Does Student Finance Count as Debt?

Student loans are technically money that you borrow and may have to repay, but they work differently from many ordinary forms of consumer borrowing.

For eligible borrowers, repayments generally become due when you have left or completed your course and your income is above the relevant repayment threshold.

The exact repayment rules depend on your student-loan plan.

This is important because a student loan shouldn't automatically be treated in the same way as a credit card balance or personal loan.

However, students should still understand their borrowing and avoid assuming that all forms of credit are harmless simply because they already have student finance.

Student Finance and Other Debt

A student might have:

  • Student loan
  • Student overdraft
  • Credit card
  • Buy Now Pay Later balance
  • Personal loan
  • Phone contract
  • Rent arrears
  • Council tax arrears after leaving university
  • Other household or personal debts

These are not all treated in the same way.

For example, using an overdraft every month to cover a permanent shortfall may indicate that your income isn't covering your essential expenses.

Similarly, repeatedly using Buy Now Pay Later to pay for food, bills or other necessities can be a warning sign that your budget needs attention.

If you already have several debts outside your student finance, you can explore the debt solutions and financial support available through The Credit Advice before taking on additional borrowing.

How Students Can Manage Money Better

1. Create a Monthly Budget

Start with your total expected income.

Then calculate your essential expenses.

For example:

Monthly income – essential expenses = money available for flexible spending

Don't forget irregular expenses such as:

  • University trips
  • Course materials
  • Birthdays
  • Travel home
  • Clothing
  • Deposits
  • Annual subscriptions

2. Divide Your Maintenance Loan Into Monthly Amounts

Maintenance payments are often made in instalments rather than as a single monthly salary.

A common mistake is spending heavily immediately after receiving an instalment.

Instead, divide the payment across the period it needs to cover.

For example:

£3,000 received ÷ 4 months = £750 per month

This doesn't mean you must spend exactly £750 every month. It simply gives you a clearer spending framework.

3. Keep an Emergency Buffer

Unexpected expenses happen.

A broken laptop, emergency journey home or unexpected bill can quickly disrupt a student budget.

Even a small emergency fund can provide some breathing room.

4. Be Careful With Credit

Credit can be useful in certain situations, but using borrowing to pay for everyday necessities can become expensive or difficult to manage.

Before applying for a credit card, overdraft or personal loan, ask:

"Can I afford the repayments from my normal income?"

If the answer is no, borrowing may only postpone the underlying problem.

5. Look for Student Discounts

Reducing expenses is often easier than increasing income.

Students may have access to discounts on:

  • Transport
  • Software
  • Food
  • Entertainment
  • Clothing
  • Technology
  • Gym memberships

Small savings can add up over an academic year.

What If Your Student Finance Isn't Enough?

This is one of the most important questions students should ask.

If your Maintenance Loan doesn't cover your essential living costs, don't immediately assume that another loan is the answer.

Consider:

University Support

Speak to your university's student support or finance team. Some institutions have hardship funds, bursaries or other forms of assistance.

Part-Time Work

A part-time job may help increase income, although students should make sure employment doesn't interfere with their studies.

Review Your Budget

Look at your largest expenses first.

For many students, accommodation is one of the biggest costs, so reducing housing or travel expenses can sometimes make a larger difference than cutting small purchases.

Can Student Debt Affect Your Credit Score?

It's important to distinguish between a student loan and other forms of borrowing.

A student loan isn't simply the same as a credit card or personal loan appearing on your credit file.

However, other debts you take out while studying can have consequences if payments are missed.

For example, missed payments on ordinary credit can potentially affect your credit history.

This is another reason why students should avoid taking on credit simply to maintain a lifestyle they cannot otherwise afford.

Student Finance in England vs Other UK Nations

The UK doesn't have one identical student finance system.

Students in:

  • England
  • Scotland
  • Wales
  • Northern Ireland

may have different funding arrangements.

If you're unsure which rules apply to you, check the official government or relevant student finance body for your nation before making financial decisions.

For England, GOV.UK provides information on eligibility, applications and student finance options.

Student Finance Changes From 2027

Students should also be aware that the UK higher-education funding system is changing.

For many courses starting on or after 1 January 2027, the new Lifelong Learning Entitlement (LLE) system will apply. GOV.UK says LLE is intended to replace previous higher-education and some further-education funding arrangements for eligible courses.

This makes it particularly important to check the current government rules rather than relying on information from previous academic years.

When Should a Student Ask for Debt Help?

You don't have to wait until your debts become overwhelming.

Consider getting help if:

  • You're regularly using credit to pay for food or bills.
  • You only make minimum payments on credit cards.
  • Your overdraft is constantly at its limit.
  • You're borrowing money to repay other borrowing.
  • You're receiving letters about missed payments.
  • You have fallen behind with essential bills.
  • You don't know how much you owe.
  • You feel unable to keep up with repayments.

The earlier you understand the situation, the more clearly you can explore your options.

Frequently Asked Questions About Student Finance UK

Is student finance available to everyone?

No. Eligibility depends on factors including your course, previous study, nationality, residency, age and other circumstances.

What is the difference between a Tuition Fee Loan and Maintenance Loan?

A Tuition Fee Loan helps cover eligible tuition fees and is normally paid directly to your university. A Maintenance Loan is intended to help with living costs and is normally paid into your bank account.

How much is the Maintenance Loan in 2026?

There isn't one single amount for everyone. The amount depends on circumstances such as household income, where you live and your study arrangements. GOV.UK provides the current figures and eligibility criteria.

Do I have to repay my student loan immediately after university?

No. Repayment normally depends on your student-loan plan and whether your income is above the relevant repayment threshold.

Can I use my Maintenance Loan for rent?

Yes. A Maintenance Loan is intended to help eligible students with living costs, which can include accommodation and other everyday expenses.

Final Thoughts: Make Student Finance Work for You

Student finance can make university more accessible, but understanding your money is just as important as receiving it.

The best starting point is a simple budget.

Know how much money is coming in, understand your essential costs and avoid using expensive credit to repeatedly cover a gap between income and spending.

If you're already dealing with credit card balances, overdrafts, unpaid bills or other financial commitments alongside your student finance, don't wait until the situation becomes impossible to manage.
